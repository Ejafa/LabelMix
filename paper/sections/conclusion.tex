\section{Conclusion}

We introduced TreemapMix, a Dirichlet-controlled $K$-way image-mixing
augmentation that composes multiple labeled images into area-proportional
treemap layouts while preserving the training sample count. By generating
cyclic views of each source group, TreemapMix exposes each source image to
different region sizes and rank positions, enabling two complementary
supervision objectives: area-weighted soft cross-entropy for probability
matching and Plackett-Luce ranking for area-induced ordinal supervision.

Empirically, TreemapMix improves ImageNet-1K classification under matched
training recipes. TreemapMix-SCE gives the strongest calibration, while
TreemapMix-PL gives the strongest Top-1 accuracy and improves area-logit
alignment. COCO transfer experiments further suggest that TreemapMix-pretrained
representations remain useful for detection and instance segmentation.
The accuracy--calibration pattern also extends to the additional ImageNet-1K
architectures and largely to CIFAR-100. On ImageNet-LT, PL improves accuracy,
but SCE loses its calibration advantage. Loader measurements quantify the
additional host-side throughput and memory cost.

Despite these improvements, TreemapMix relies on class-diverse source groups
and is most effective when mixed images contain distinct target labels.
Repeated labels require area aggregation, which can weaken the Plackett-Luce
signal, especially on imbalanced or long-tailed datasets. TreemapMix also
introduces composition hyperparameters such as $K$ and $\alpha$, motivating
future work on adaptive grouping, automatic schedules, and extensions to
contrastive or self-supervised learning.
