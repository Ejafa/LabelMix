\section{Diagnostic and Transfer Analysis}
We next examine how TreemapMix behaves beyond aggregate classification
accuracy. The analyses in this section support two main observations. First,
the PL objective improves alignment between model logits and the area-induced
ordering of the composed regions. Second, COCO transfer experiments indicate
that the learned representations remain useful for downstream instance
segmentation, although TreemapMix is not designed as a detection-specific
augmentation. We analyze these effects through compositional logit alignment
and COCO transfer, while deferring the hyperparameter sweep to
Appendix~\ref{app:in1k-hparam-sensitivity} and the qualitative attribution
analysis to Appendix~\ref{app:qualitative-visualizations}.

\paragraph{Compositional Logit Alignment:}\label{sec:logit-alignment}
Because TreemapMix composes each training sample from multiple source images, a
natural question is whether the resulting models learn logits that reflect the
relative contribution of each constituent region. We test this by measuring
whether class logits align with the known area ordering in composed images.

We evaluate the ViT-Betwixt checkpoints from the main ImageNet-1K
experiments reported in Figure~\ref{fig:in1k-main-results-top1} across three
seeds on a held-out composed diagnostic set. The set contains 500 TreemapMix
samples for each
$K \in \{3,4,5,6\}$. Figure~\ref{fig:area-logit-diagnostic} reports three
diagnostics as separate panels: present-class pairwise rank accuracy over
pairs satisfying $|\Delta\,\mathrm{area}| \ge 0.05$ in
the left heatmap, area-logit Spearman correlation in the center heatmap, and
largest-area top-class accuracy in the right heatmap.

\input{fig/fig_area_logit_diagnostic}

As expected for a ranking-based objective, TreemapMix-PL yields the strongest
present-class rank accuracy in the left heatmap of
Figure~\ref{fig:area-logit-diagnostic} and the strongest area-logit Spearman
correlation in the center heatmap. TreemapMix-SCE does not match
TreemapMix-PL on these diagnostics and, in some cases, trails RICAP. This
suggests that compositional augmentation alone is not sufficient to induce the
same ranking behavior; the PL objective is important for aligning logits with
the area-induced ordering.

A similar pattern appears for largest-area top-class accuracy in the right
heatmap of Figure~\ref{fig:area-logit-diagnostic}. TreemapMix-PL achieves the
highest largest-area top-class accuracy, indicating that it aligns logits most
closely with relative patch area. In contrast, TreemapMix-SCE is less
consistent and often remains closer to the baselines. As in the previous experiments, TreemapMix-mixed typically falls between TreemapMix-PL and TreemapMix-SCE on both ECE and Top-1 accuracy. RICAP is nevertheless a
competitive baseline across all three metrics and outperforms TreemapMix-SCE on
largest-area top-class accuracy and area-logit Spearman correlation, despite
also using area-weighted soft targets. This further suggests that the
strongest gains in these diagnostics are driven by the ranking objective rather
than by compositional augmentation alone.

\paragraph{Feature Transfer to Instance Segmentation:}

We next test whether TreemapMix-pretrained checkpoints remain useful beyond
ImageNet-1K classification. The logit-alignment diagnostics in
Section~\ref{sec:logit-alignment} show that TreemapMix-PL more strongly aligns
present-class logits with the area-induced ordering of the composed regions.
This diagnostic does not establish that TreemapMix learns better dense spatial
features. We therefore treat object detection and instance segmentation as
downstream transfer tests rather than as causal evidence for the mechanism
behind the logit-alignment results.

Our goal is not to propose a new object detector or a detection-specific
augmentation. Instead, we use COCO transfer to compare the representations
learned by different ImageNet-1K pretraining augmentations under a fixed
fine-tuning setup. We evaluate ViT-Wee and ViT-Betwixt to cover smaller and
larger backbones. Following the ViTDet setup
\citep{liExploringPlainVision2022}, we use a ViTDet-style adapter and train a
Mask R-CNN detector \citep{liExploringPlainVision2022,heMaskRCNN2018}. We
fine-tune on COCO for 30 epochs, initializing from the checkpoints produced in
the main classification experiments. COCO images are resized to
256$\times$256 pixels to match the input resolution used during ImageNet-1K
pretraining.

We evaluate TreemapMix-SCE and TreemapMix-PL and compare them with
Mixup+CutMix-pretrained and RICAP-pretrained baselines
\citep{takahashiDataAugmentationUsing2020}. We include RICAP because it is the
closest multi-image composition baseline in our study and is related to
Mosaic-style object-detection augmentations used in YOLOv4 and YOLOv5
\citep{bochkovskiyYOLOv4OptimalSpeed2020,jocherYOLOv5Ultralytics2020}. The
corresponding fine-tuning hyperparameters are reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:det-hparams}. Table~\ref{tab:coco-transfer-results} reports
COCO box AP and mask AP, averaged over three transferred checkpoints whose
ImageNet-1K pretraining used different random seeds. The appendix gives the
transfer setup and its hyperparameters.

\input{fig/tab_coco_transfer_results}

Both TreemapMix variants improve over Mixup+CutMix and RICAP on box AP and
mask AP for both backbones, with TreemapMix-PL achieving the highest scores.
These results show that TreemapMix-pretrained checkpoints transfer favorably to
COCO detection and instance segmentation under this fine-tuning protocol.
However, they should not be interpreted as proving that the area-logit
alignment mechanism causes the downstream gains, since the transfer experiment
compares pretrained checkpoints rather than isolating individual representation
properties.
